1. Business Problem and Objective

Aeolus Renewables requires reliable electricity delivery under long-term power purchase agreements. Gearbox and main-bearing faults can contribute to extended outages, emergency repair costs, and additional drivetrain damage. This proof of concept classifies each SCADA record as normal (failure=0) or indicative of a current drivetrain fault (failure=1).

Failure recall is the primary criterion because a false negative is a real fault the model misses. Precision, F1, PR-AUC, ROC-AUC, alert burden, and episode recall are retained so recall is not optimized without regard to operational usability.

2. Data Overview and Exploratory Analysis

The source contains 131,760 records, 35 columns, and 15 turbines at a verified 10-minute cadence. It spans 1 January through 1 March 2024. The target contains 3,953 failure records (3.000%). Missingness is limited to gearbox-oil temperature, generator-bearing temperature, and oil pressure, each affecting approximately 0.5% of rows.

  • Failure records formed 219 per-turbine episodes in the complete dataset.
  • Failure observations showed elevated gearbox-bearing temperature, drivetrain vibration, bearing-frequency amplitude, and oil-particle count.
  • Vibration and temperature outliers were retained because they were disproportionately associated with failure records.
  • Observed relationships are associations and are not evidence of physical causation.

4. Feature Engineering

Step 04 created 24 domain-informed snapshot features and 36 past-only temporal features. These include safe operating ratios, component temperature rises, FFT summaries, circular direction encodings, lagged measurements, recent changes, and one- and three-hour rolling statistics calculated independently per turbine. No centered or future-looking windows were used.

A training/validation ablation selected 86 predictors combining original measurements with engineered features. The test set was not scored during this selection.

9. Actionable Insights and Recommendations

  1. Prioritize sustained vibration: route persistent drivetrain-vibration elevations and supporting tower/FFT patterns to analyst review.
  2. Deploy as decision support: use scores to prioritize inspections; do not trigger autonomous shutdown or maintenance without qualified review.
  3. Control alert burden: convert consecutive positive records into one alert episode and apply a documented cooldown. Validate whether the observed false-positive burden fits maintenance capacity.
  4. Investigate missed episodes: review the five undetected test episodes by turbine, duration, operating state, and sensor availability.
  5. Monitor drift: track failure prevalence, feature distributions, recall, precision, and alert volume by turbine and calendar period.
  6. Validate prospectively: conduct a shadow deployment before operational use and retrain only after appropriately labeled recent data are available.

10. Limitations and Responsible Use

  • The dataset represents 15 turbines and approximately two months, limiting seasonal and fleet-wide generalization.
  • The label supports detection of current fault conditions; it does not demonstrate hours- or days-ahead failure forecasting.
  • Failure prevalence rises materially between training and later periods.
  • Episode recall counts an episode as detected if any constituent record is positive and does not itself measure advance warning.
  • False-positive records are not equivalent to work orders until an alert aggregation policy is defined.
  • Feature importance is model-specific and associative, not causal.

11. Conclusion

The final XGBoost pipeline provides strong discrimination and detects 85.18% of failure records and 92.31% of failure episodes in the later test period. Sustained drivetrain-vibration behavior is the dominant signal family. The results support a promising analyst-triage proof of concept, subject to prospective validation, alert aggregation, ongoing drift monitoring, and qualified human review.
